You are not holding a single story, but an invitation into a landscape of symbols, fragments, and allegories. These pages are not meant to be read as straightforward fiction, nor as sacred text, but as something that lives between—parables that ask more than they answer.

The Artifacts are fragments, like broken tablets discovered in the ruins of memory. Each points to something larger than itself: a truth glimpsed, but not yet whole.

The Mask that Ate the Face turns inward, a psychological allegory about trauma and the fragile masks we learn to wear in order to survive.

The Forbidden Path turns outward, into a community bound by law and fear, where to question is to risk everything.

Taken together, these works move from fragments to self to society. They ask what it means to live with symbols, to walk with fear, and to risk freedom.

This book will not give you certainty. Instead, it will invite you to listen closely, to question deeply, and to walk the hidden paths that lie beyond the safe ones.
