This section provides supplementary information to enhance understanding of the biblical texts and their historical development.

## Historical Timeline

1. **c. 1200–1000 BCE** — Emergence of Israel; earliest poetry (Song of Deborah, Song of the Sea)
2. **c. 1000–922 BCE** — United Monarchy; development of royal narratives and court histories
3. **c. 922–722 BCE** — Divided Kingdom; composition of J and E sources
4. **c. 740–700 BCE** — First writing prophets (Amos, Hosea, Micah, Isaiah)
5. **621 BCE** — Discovery of Deuteronomy; Josiah's reform
6. **586–538 BCE** — Babylonian exile; Ezekiel, Second Isaiah, Priestly source
7. **538–400 BCE** — Persian period; return and restoration (Chronicles, Ezra-Nehemiah)
8. **400–200 BCE** — Hellenistic period; wisdom literature crisis (Job, Ecclesiastes)
9. **167–164 BCE** — Maccabean revolt; Daniel composed
10. **c. 250–150 BCE** — Septuagint translation in Alexandria
11. **c. 50–70 CE** — Paul's authentic letters; oral Jesus traditions
12. **70 CE** — Destruction of Jerusalem; Mark's Gospel
13. **80–90 CE** — Matthew and Luke
14. **90–100 CE** — John's Gospel
15. **95–100 CE** — Revelation
16. **100–150 CE** — Deutero-Pauline letters, Catholic Epistles, canon formation

## Key Archaeological Sites

- **Jerusalem** — Capital of united/divided kingdoms; excavations confirm continuous occupation and destruction levels
- **Lachish** — Judean fortress; destruction evidence from Assyrian/Babylonian conquests; Nineveh reliefs
- **Samaria** — Capital of northern kingdom; ivory decorations and ostraca reveal wealth and administration
- **Qumran** — Dead Sea Scrolls community; sectarian Judaism and manuscript preservation
- **Capernaum** — Jesus’s Galilean base; synagogue and house excavations
- **Ephesus** — Major Pauline and Johannine center; extensive ruins show early Christian urban context

## Manuscripts and Textual Witnesses

- **Dead Sea Scrolls** (c. 250 BCE–70 CE) — Hebrew/Aramaic manuscripts including biblical, sectarian, and apocryphal writings
- **Codex Sinaiticus** (4th c. CE) — Earliest complete Greek New Testament
- **Codex Vaticanus** (4th c. CE) — Nearly complete Greek Bible, key textual witness
- **Chester Beatty Papyri** (2nd–3rd c. CE) — Early fragments of New Testament books
- **Nag Hammadi Library** (4th c. CE) — Gnostic Christian texts in Coptic, alternative early Christianity

## Scholarly Frameworks

- **Documentary Hypothesis** — Pentateuch compiled from J, E, D, P sources
- **Q Source** — Hypothetical sayings collection used by Matthew and Luke
- **Synoptic Problem** — Literary relationships among Matthew, Mark, Luke; Markan priority + Q
- **Deuteronomistic History** — Joshua–Kings as unified theological history
- **Johannine Community** — Distinctive sectarian origins of John’s Gospel, letters, Revelation

## Languages and Translation

- **Hebrew** — From archaic to late biblical Hebrew
- **Aramaic** — Persian-era lingua franca; portions of Daniel and Ezra; spoken by Jesus
- **Greek** — Septuagint and New Testament written in Koine Greek
- **Translation Issues** — Theological development shaped by language shifts

## Social and Historical Contexts

- **Agriculture & Trade** — Farming-based society; trade routes shaped prosperity
- **Imperial Systems** — Egyptian, Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Greek, Roman contexts
- **Social Stratification** — Elites vs. common people; prophets critique inequality
- **Temple-Centered Religion** — Political/economic hub until destructions of 586 & 70 CE
- **Diaspora Judaism** — Identity maintained beyond the Temple
- **Religious Diversity** — Judaism and early Christianity had wide theological variety

## Literary Genres

- **Historical Narratives** — Theological storytelling, not modern history
- **Prophetic Literature** — Social critique, covenant, judgment/restoration
- **Wisdom Literature** — Instruction, philosophy, reflections on justice
- **Apocalyptic** — Symbolic visions of cosmic conflict and victory
- **Epistles** — Letters addressing community situations
- **Gospels** — Hybrid of biography, theology, and instruction

## Theological Developments

- **Monotheism** — From monolatry to philosophical monotheism
- **Covenant Theology** — Conditional divine-human relationship
- **Messiah/Christ** — From royal ideology to Christian doctrine
- **Resurrection** — From corporate restoration to individual afterlife hope
- **Canon Formation** — Gradual recognition of authoritative texts (completed by 4th c. CE)

## Methods of Study

- **Historical-Critical** — Origins, development, context of texts
- **Archaeological** — Material culture to test/confirm claims
- **Literary Criticism** — Genres, structures, artistry
- **Social-Scientific** — Anthropology/sociology applied to ancient societies
- **Canonical Criticism** — How texts function in larger collections

## Notes on Usage

- **Dating** — All dates approximate; scholarly debate continues. BCE = Before Common Era, CE = Common Era.
- **Terminology** — “Hebrew Bible” and “Old Testament” used interchangeably; “Israel” may mean people, kingdom, or land depending on context.
