I am grateful to my family and friends, who tolerated countless hours of writing, research, and revision. Their patience, encouragement, and steady presence sustained me through periods of exhaustion and uncertainty. More than once, their gentle reminders of life beyond the manuscript helped restore balance and perspective.

This book also owes much to the wider community of biblical scholars whose work precedes mine. Their rigorous engagement with ancient languages, their willingness to confront difficult texts, and their commitment to intellectual honesty provided both models and foundations. Whatever merits this work possesses arise from standing on their shoulders.

Finally, I extend my gratitude to the countless voices of the past—scribes, storytellers, translators, and communities—whose words became the material of this study. Though separated by centuries and cultures, their questions about justice, meaning, and the divine still speak into our present age. It is a humbling honor to listen to them and to join my voice, however modestly, in that enduring conversation. Any errors or shortcomings that remain are entirely my own.
