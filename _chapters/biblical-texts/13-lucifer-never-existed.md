The word "Lucifer" appears only once in the Bible

(Isaiah 14:12) and it's a mistranslation of "Helel ben Shachar," meaning

"shining one, son of the dawn."

It was originally about the king of Babylon and his arrogance and downfall, not about an angel.

The idea of Lucifer as a rebellious angel came from much later Christian misinterpretation.

Ezekiel 28:2-10

is often cited as describing Lucifer, however scholars agree it is explicitly talking about the king of Tyre, not a rebellious angel. So how did we come to these misunderstandings, well today our Bibles are based off of the mosaic version, where as the uncut and unfiltered scriptures come from the predecessor the Septuagint, that begs the question

Scholarly Note: In Christian tradition, 'Lucifer' has often been associated with Satan, drawing on interpretations of Isaiah 14 and other texts.

However, in Jewish tradition and in many modern academic analyses, the term in its original context refers to the Babylonian king or a metaphorical figure.
