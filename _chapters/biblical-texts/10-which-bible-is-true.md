WHICH BIBLE IS TRUE?

Bibles we see today are derivatives of what in known as the mosaic Bible which was a translation and alteration from the Septuagint and

The Septuagint was the authoritative Greek translation of

Hebrew Scriptures, completed around 285–247 BCE in

Alexandria by seventy Jewish scholars. This was the Bible that

Jesus quoted from, that Paul referenced in his letters, and that the early Christian church considered scripture.

The Dead Sea Scrolls (200 BCE–70 CE) reveal that Second Temple Judaism used texts closely matching the Septuagint tradition—not the later Hebrew manuscripts that would become our modern Old Testament. These ancient discoveries are interpreted by some scholars to suggest the Septuagint preserved earlier textual traditions according to some researchers.

Textual Manipulation

What happened next was textual modification. Jewish scribes, working centuries after Christ, created the Masoretic Text—a heavily edited, standardized version that intentionally diverged according to certain scholarly interpretations from the Septuagint to distance Judaism from Christian interpretations. Variations were eliminated, prophecies were altered, and entire passages were modified.

This altered Masoretic version became the foundation for virtually every Bible sitting in churches today. The King James Bible (1611) primarily focuses on these later, adulterated Hebrew texts rather than the authentic Septuagint tradition used by the apostles themselves.

Modern translations—the NIV, ESV, NASB, and the New World Translation—all trace their Old Testament lineage back to these same later standardized manuscripts, not to the Bible that Jesus and the early church actually used.

We've been reading a rewrite of a rewrite, while the original text used by Christ himself sits largely ignored in scholarly archives.
