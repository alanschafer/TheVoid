Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John are not eyewitness accounts. They were not written until decades after the events they describe, and the authors were not present at the events described; the names associated were not added to them until later.

The Gospels being the exclusive portrayal of Jesus what they actually offer is a contradictory version of the same story—one that doesn't really agree with itself on anything, including facts, timelines, geography, genealogy, and resurrection

For example:

Mark is the earliest Gospel written around (70 AD) but the shortest. It ends abruptly, with the shortest manuscript having no post-resurrection appearance (Mark 16:8).

Both Matthew (80-85 CE) and Luke (80-85 CE)

borrow heavily from Mark, sometimes copying it word for word, with some contradictions. Matthew 5-7

describes the Sermon on the Mount, specifically on a mountainside.

While Luke 6:17 describes the Sermon on a plain, specifically described as a level place.

Matthew traces Jesus' bloodline through Joseph to Abraham (Matthew 1:1–16).

But Luke traces it to Adam (Luke 3:23–38).

John, written last (90–100 AD),

portrays Jesus as a more mystical, divine, and philosophical version who speaks in abstract monologues. Compared to the abrupt, cryptic parables in Matthew, Mark & Luke.
